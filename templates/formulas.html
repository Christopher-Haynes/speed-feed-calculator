Formulas

Every number on the calculator comes from these formulas. Inch and metric versions are both shown; the calculator works in inch and converts.

Spindle speed (RPM)

How fast the spindle turns so the cutting edge moves past the material at the right surface speed.

RPM = (SFM × 12) ÷ (π × D) D in inches RPM = (Vc × 1000) ÷ (π × D) Vc in m/min, D in mm

12 ÷ π is about 3.82, which is where the shop rule RPM = SFM × 3.82 ÷ D comes from.

  • Milling and drilling: D is the tool diameter.
  • Turning: D is the part diameter, because the part is what spins.

RPM limits

RPM is capped at the lowest limit: the machine max, and on a lathe the chuck or workholding max. When RPM is capped, the real surface speed drops:

SFM = RPM × π × D ÷ 12

Feed is always figured from the capped RPM. Using the uncapped RPM would put a bigger chip on every tooth than planned.

Feed rate

Milling: feed = RPM × chip load × flutes Drilling: feed = RPM × feed per rev Turning: feed = RPM × feed per rev

Radial chip thinning

When the radial depth of cut (WOC) is less than half the tool diameter, each tooth takes a thinner chip than the programmed chip load. Too thin and the tool rubs instead of cutting. To get the intended chip back, the chip load is multiplied by:

factor = 1 ÷ √(1 − (1 − 2 × WOC ÷ D)²)

At 50% WOC or more the factor is 1 (no change). At 25% it's about 1.15, and at 10% it's about 1.67. You can turn the adjustment off to see how thin the chip really gets.

Ball end mill effective diameter

At an axial depth under half the diameter, a ball mill only cuts with a smaller circle near its tip:

D effective = 2 × √(DOC × (D − DOC))

The real surface speed at that diameter is lower than at the full diameter, and the calculator notes it.

Circular interpolation

The machine moves the tool center, but the chip is made at the edge. On an arc those travel different distances, so the programmed feed is adjusted:

Inside a hole: F center = F edge × (D hole − D tool) ÷ D hole Around a boss: F center = F edge × (D part + D tool) ÷ D part

Constant surface speed on a lathe (G96 / G50)

G96 S sets the surface speed, and the control raises RPM as the diameter gets smaller. G50 S caps the RPM, which matters most when facing or parting toward center. Set it to the lowest of the machine, chuck and workholding limits. The cap is reached at:

D = SFM × 12 ÷ (π × RPM limit)

Below that diameter the spindle sits at the cap and surface speed drops. Some controls use a different code for the RPM clamp (for example G92 on some Fanuc G-code systems), so check your control's manual.

Threading (G97)

Threading runs at a fixed RPM (G97), not constant surface speed, so the feed stays in step with the spindle. The feed per rev is the thread lead:

Inch: lead = 1 ÷ TPI Metric: lead = pitch (mm)

For multi-start threads, lead = pitch × number of starts.

Material removal rate (MRR)

Milling: MRR = WOC × DOC × feed Drilling: MRR = (π ÷ 4) × D² × feed Turning: MRR = 12 × SFM × feed per rev × DOC in³/min Turning: MRR = Vc × feed per rev × DOC cm³/min (metric)

Horsepower check

Each material needs a certain power to remove one cubic inch per minute (its unit power). Where the data has it:

HP at the cutter = MRR × unit power

The spindle motor needs more than this, because of losses in the machine, so treat a result close to your spindle rating as a warning sign.
